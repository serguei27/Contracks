The Delivery Control Ladder™

How early do you see it? How quickly do you act?

The ladder is a maturity model for delivery control. It has five levels, from Reactive to Scalable, and it measures two things: how early you detect an issue, and how quickly you take effective action.

Why it matters. Most programmes look controlled until pressure arrives. The higher the level, the earlier you see a risk, while it’s still a gap and not yet a claim, and the faster you act on it. The work is the same. What changes is when you find out and what happens next.

The ladder shows where your project stands. The 5Cs are how you climb: contain the scope, command one real plan, close decisions, confirm commitments, control the weekly rhythm.

What each level looks like.

1

Reactive

See: After the damageAct: Firefight

What it means
Controls live on paper, not on the programme. Problems are discovered after they happen, and delivery depends on heroics and firefighting.
What breaks
Dashboards show green while the work drifts. Nobody sees the surprise coming until it is already a crisis.
On a real programme
A delivery slips. You find out when the next trade stops.
2

Managed

See: In reports, after the driftAct: Respond late

What it means
A governance framework is in place, but the live programme runs off-system. Reports are produced; control is not consistent.
What breaks
Two plans run at once and only one is real. Slippage shows up in last week’s numbers.
On a real programme
The deck says green. You call the site, and the site says something else.
3

Controlled

See: While the gap emergesAct: Assign, decide and resolve

What it means
Scope, plan, decisions, vendor dates and cadence are actively managed. This is where the 5Cs are installed and working.
What breaks
Control holds, but it isn’t yet predictive. You catch issues early, but you aren’t forecasting them.
On a real programme
The late delivery comes up at this week’s review, with an owner and a recovery date.
4

Operational

See: Before the impactAct: Prevent or recover

What it means
Risks are visible early. Recovery actions trigger before schedule and cost impacts land. The programme moves on facts, not optimism.
What breaks
The discipline still leans on the current team. It isn’t yet a system that survives turnover and scale.
On a real programme
A vendor date fails its capacity check. The plan changes before the date reaches the baseline.
5

Scalable

See: Early, across projectsAct: Anticipate and adapt consistently

What it means
Very little reaches the board by accident. Issues are identified, owned and resolved before they escalate. The programme is evidence-driven.
What breaks
The only risk left is complacency: protecting the system as the programme scales and the team turns over.
On a real programme
A new country opens on the same system, and the local team runs it without head office in the room.

The “on a real programme” lines are illustrations, not case results.

The 5Cs Framework™

The 5Cs are how you climb.

The ladder tells you where you stand. The 5Cs show you how to improve. Each discipline strengthens your ability to detect problems earlier, make decisions faster and keep delivery under control. Maturity increases as these disciplines become consistent, measurable and independent of individual heroes.

Contain the Scope

Written scope, documented assumptions, clear acceptance criteria. Every change goes through one gate.

Command One Plan

One live baseline that matches the site. Not parallel plans nobody trusts.

Close Every Decision

Every open item gets an owner, a deadline and a consequence.

Confirm Vendor Dates

Capacity and lead times checked before a date enters the baseline.

Control Tower Cadence

A fixed weekly rhythm that catches slippage early.
